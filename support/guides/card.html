To charge it, the side switch must be ON.
Plug in a switched-off Cardputer and nothing happens — no light, no charge, and it looks broken. It isn't. Switch it on first, then plug in. The left top trigger, BTN RST, restarts the device and loses nothing.

Left to right walks up the scale, and every key is a note of it — A minor pentatonic out of the box — so there are no dead keys and nothing to avoid. Each row behaves like a string: one voice, gliding between whatever you press on it.

The sliding is the instrument
hold one key · press another on the same row

Hammer-on

The voice glides to the new note instead of restarting. No new attack, no click — one continuous line, like sliding a finger up a string.

then let the second one go

Pull-off

It glides back down to the key you're still holding. Together they give you trills, grace notes, and the scoops that make a line sound played rather than typed.

hold a shape across rows · re-finger it elsewhere

Slide a chord — this is the thing

Hold two or three keys across different rows, then move that shape while the old notes still ring. Every voice glides to its new home at once — the gesture no keyboard can make, and why the instrument exists.

The sounds are yours
Ten sit on fn+q..p. The last two were rolled from a seed made on your device's first boot — no other unit on Earth has them. tab → Randomize rolls more.
One hand backs, one solos
The bottom row latches: tap a key and it drones an octave down, hands-free. alt is the loop pedal, recording the performance — so a new sound re-voices the loop.
What the screen says
The waveform is the real output. Top left the sound's name, top middle the key and scale, then OCT, vox the voices sounding, * unsaved edits.
Nothing you make is at risk
Slots, settings and card files live outside the program, so a new GLIDE cannot touch them. The whole manual is on the device: tab → help.
