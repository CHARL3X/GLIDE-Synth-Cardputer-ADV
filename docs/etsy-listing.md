# Selling GLIDE units — the Etsy listing

Working copy for the shop listing, plus the things that have to be true about
each box before it goes out. Everything in a `text` block is meant to be copied
straight into Etsy. Placeholders in `{{BRACES}}` are yours to fill.

---

## 1. What has to be in every box

Three of these are licence obligations, not politeness. The firmware links MIT,
BSD, Apache-2.0 and LGPL-2.1 code, and every one of those licences says the
notices travel with the binary. Selling a flashed device is distributing the
binary.

| In the box | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Cardputer with Launcher flashed, `GLIDE.bin` in `/apps/` on the card | The product |
| `NOTICES.txt` copied to the root of the microSD card | MIT / BSD / Apache / LGPL attribution |
| Printed insert card (section 8) with the notices link + LGPL offer | So the buyer can find it without a card reader |
| The word "unofficial / not affiliated with M5Stack" somewhere visible | Trademark hygiene |

`dist/NOTICES.txt` is generated by `python support/gen_notices.py` and refreshed
on every `pio run`. Copy the current one onto each card; don't hand-edit it.

**Before the first unit ships:** rebuild from a tree whose `cfg::kVersion` is
**not** `v2.5`. Releases v2.5 and earlier went out under GPL-3.0 and stay there
— a unit whose splash reads v2.5 is arguing the buyer's copy is the GPL one,
which would let anyone redistribute and sell it. The tree is on v2.6 now; it
needs a `pio run` so `dist/GLIDE.bin` matches, and a v2.6 release tag.

between. The first ~160 characters are the search preview, so they carry weight.

## 5. Variations and pricing

Two variations, because they are genuinely different instruments:

| Variation | Difference to say in the option name |
| --- | --- |
| **Cardputer ADV** | "ADV — includes the listen-and-retune microphone feature" |
| **Cardputer v1.1** | "v1.1 — everything except mic key detection" |

Price the ADV higher than the v1.1 by more than the hardware difference: it is
the model where the headline demo feature works.

Work the floor out rather than guessing at it:

```
floor = hardware + microSD + packaging + shipping
      + (your build time × your rate)
      + Etsy's cut: $0.20 listing + 6.5% transaction + ~3% + $0.25 payment
      + the offsite-ads fee if it applies to you (12% or 15%, and it is not
        optional above the revenue threshold)
```

Every unit costs you real money and an hour of attention, and the DM backlog in
your inbox is evidence the demand is not price-sensitive yet. Do not price at
the floor. If you sell out, raise it; a queue is not a reason to work for less.

Set **processing time honestly** — you are flashing and testing each one by
hand. 3–5 business days beats 1 and a pile of "where is it" messages. Etsy's
quantity field is your friend: list the number you can actually ship this week,
not the number you ordered.

## 6. Shipping and policies

- **Lithium battery.** The Cardputer has one. Ship it *installed in the device*
  (that's the easy category), ground services domestically, and check the
  carrier's rules before promising international. Some destinations and some
  services will refuse it. Say so in the listing rather than in an apology
  later.
- **Returns.** Offer them on a working device, 30 days, buyer pays return
  shipping. Excluding "I changed my mind" is fine; excluding "it arrived dead"
  is not, and consumer law says so regardless of what the policy field says.
- **Warranty.** Say what you'll actually do: "If it arrives faulty or fails in
  {{N}} days, message me and I'll replace it." The software's as-is disclaimer
  covers the firmware; it does not cover the physical product you sold.
- **What you're not promising.** No liability for hearing damage at volume, no
  guarantee against a buyer flashing something else over it. One line each.

## 7. Etsy FAQ entries

Copy these into the listing's FAQ section — every one is a DM you'd otherwise
answer by hand.

```
Q: Do I need a computer, an app, or WiFi?
A: No. It arrives working. Charge it, switch it on, play. Updates are optional
   and are a single file you copy onto the card.

Q: Can I hear it?
A: Yes — demo videos are in the listing photos, and there are more on the
   project page. Every device sounds slightly different, so treat them as an
   example rather than a promise.

Q: Do I need to know music theory?
A: No. Scale lock means every key you press is in key. Holding shift is how you
   step outside it, once you want to.

Q: How is this different from downloading the free firmware myself?
A: It isn't, functionally — the firmware is free and open source, and if you
   already own a Cardputer you should just download it. You're buying a working
   instrument instead of a project evening.

Q: What's the difference between the ADV and the v1.1?
A: The ADV has a microphone, so it can listen to a song playing in the room and
   retune itself to that song's key. Everything else is the same.

Q: Can I put my own sounds on it?
A: Yes. Roll and save unlimited sounds to the SD card as named files, and move
   them between devices. Ten live slots, and a card library behind them.

Q: Will it get updates?
A: Yes, free, indefinitely, and your saved sounds survive them.

Q: Is it loud enough to play with other people?
A: It has a small built-in speaker and a headphone jack. For a room, run it into
   a speaker or interface through the jack.
```

## 9. Photo and video shot list

Etsy allows 10 photos and a video. The video sells this product; a still photo
of a small grey handheld does not.

1. **Hero:** in a hand, screen lit, thumb mid-slide. Plain background.
2. **Video (do this first):** the chord slide, 8 seconds, with sound. The single
   move that explains the instrument.
3. Scale reference — beside a phone or a cassette.
4. The screen close enough to read a sound name.
5. What's in the box, laid flat: device, card, insert.
6. The keymap graphic (`assets/glide-keymap.svg`, exported to PNG).
7. Tilt in motion — hands leaning the device, blur is fine.
8. A "no two alike" card: two units side by side with different sound names.
9. Text overlay of the three headline features, for the grid thumbnail.
10. Packaging as it will arrive.

Shoot your own hardware. Don't use M5Stack's product photography.
